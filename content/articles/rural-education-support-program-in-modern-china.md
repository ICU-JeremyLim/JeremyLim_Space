Introduction

Education is critical in alleviating poverty and promoting social development, particularly in rural areas. For instance, in 1905, Samuel Pollard, a British [Methodist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Methodism) missionary, arrived in Shimenkan, a remote and mountainous village in Weining County,  Guizhou province, populated by the Miao ethnic minority, and established Christian schools. (Tan, n.d.)  Through the introduction of Western education and the creation of a writing system based on the local dialect, most of the people in Shimenkan became literate and there are many famous talents, including scholars, doctors, and soccer players from this remote tiny village in the last century, resulting in the prosperity and advantaged socioeconomic situation, which demonstrated the importance of rural education support.  

Another example is the Teach for China project (TFC). (Teach for China, 2024) Despite China's rapid economic growth in the recent 40 years, rural students still face challenges, including limited access to quality education, a lack of trained teachers, and outdated learning resources. To address these issues, Lide Future Zhuxue Commonweal Foundation, inspired by the Teach for All project (TFA), then started TFC to recruit graduated university students to support rural education and helped 1,104,531 students to accept better education. However, the low retention rate of teachers in the TFC project and the scale of this project, covering only 523 schools in rural areas, indicate that this project is not the ultimate solution to the educational disparity in rural areas in China.

Inspired by the One Laptop per Child Project (OLPC), a project aimed at transforming education for children worldwide by creating and distributing educational devices, it is possible to solve this problem through modern technologies, such as online education and artificial intelligence. According to data on the usage rate of online education in China, the usage rate soared from 27.2% (2019) to 46.8% (2020) due to the pandemic and is still kept at a high level nowadays. (SEO Agency China, 2023)  This number indicates that online education is a highly feasible and acceptable strategy to bridge the gap in educational resources between urban and rural areas, as long as the facilities for internet access are available in rural areas.

Therefore, the Rural Education Support Program in modern China aims to break the cycle of poverty by bridging the education gap by introducing modern technology and a few high-quality teachers, covering compulsory education in rural areas. This paper will outline the description of this project, the monitoring and evaluation of this project, as well as the limitations and potential challenges. 

  

Project Description

The description of rural education support in modern China projects will include the finance, the target audiences, the length of the project, and the detailed activities. (Locations, Goals, and Reasons for this project were mentioned in the introduction.)

Based on the national condition of the People's Republic of China, such large-scale educational project need the approval of the Ministry of Education and the support of the Communist Youth League of China. Therefore, the basic idea is to establish a foundation for modern rural education support based on collaboration with official organizations and receive funds from the Ministry of Education and investment from NGOs or individuals.  The money will be used for purchasing facilities, hiring teachers, and financial support.

The target audience would be primary and secondary students in rural areas of China. As for the first step, it is effective to choose some schools in disadvantaged areas for the experiment of this project, such as Yunnan Province or Guizhou Province. In the pilot phase (approximately three years), executing this project on a small scale and detecting some potential problems, covering the usage of modern technology in education, the effectiveness of this project, the retention rate of teachers, and the sustainability. The next step is to alleviate the issues found in the pilot phase, expand this project into more and more areas, and make it a long-term stable project to tackle the education disparity issue in rural areas.

The detailed activities of this project include providing hardware and software for online education, recruiting high-quality teachers, and financial assistance. First, a Wi-Fi router should be installed at each school, and each student should be equipped with a laptop to ensure internet accessibility for rural students. The costs of those facilities are acceptable because China's network coverage was basically complete during the recent ten years, and the cost of domestically produced routers and laptops is affordable for this project. (People’s Daily, 2024)

Second, designing a system for the computer, including holistic learning software and materials, is the key point of this project. For example, games can be used to learn English vocabulary and visualize math theorems. Moreover, advanced teachers in urban areas could record their classes to explain knowledge of their own subjects and upload them to a free platform so that each student could acquire knowledge through online videos and learn at their own pace. Another helpful technology is artificial intelligence (AI). AI chatbots can immediately and specifically answer any questions from students, ensuring students have a better and deeper understanding of the material. In addition, exploring the internet by themselves would result in better developing their creativity and critical thinking. In this way, teachers' workload is decreased drastically because the biggest part of teachers’ work is preparing the lectures, correcting homework, and explaining knowledge to students. Therefore, the lack of teachers could be solved this way.

Thirdly, this project still requires some high-quality teachers. Similar to TFA and TFC, this project will recruit and train teachers from graduated colleague students. Instead of solely focusing on teaching, those teachers need to guide students appropriately using laptops and software, ensure their holistic development, including exercise and moral education, and be involved in the local community. As mentioned above, the support of modern technology would free teachers from the burden of teaching, but it does not mean that allowing students to study with a laptop is sufficient. According to the evaluation of the OLPC project (Ames 2016), there are many misuse of laptops among students, such as watching porn or violent video and being addicted to computer games. Therefore, to guide students using the device for learning properly is highly necessary. This project will help all teachers acquire knowledge and skills of technology proficiency to monitor modern technology use in their classes. On the other hand, those teachers' responsibility is to stimulate students' well-rounded development. Quality education covers not only the acquisition of knowledge but also social, emotional, mental, and physical development. Thus, teachers should do what AI or software cannot do, which is to encourage students to exercise more, enhance their collaboration, and stimulate their critical thinking skills through face-to-face teaching and organizing diverse events. Moreover, teaching in rural areas requires involvement in the local community. Unless teachers let the parents of students know the significance of education, there will be countless obstacles to the project, such as some parents not allowing their children to go to school and encouraging their children to work. Another benefit of being involved in the local community is that teachers can optimize their strategies and policies for the local school based on the local context to meet the diverse needs of students. Therefore, compared to traditional rural education support, this project needs fewer teachers but requires high-quality and responsible teachers.

Finally, financial aid is a highly effective strategy in rural education support. Inspired by the Prospera conditional cash transfer program (Prospera 2014), this project will also transfer cash to the parents of students for their nutrition, hygiene, and scholarship under certain conditions. Those conditions include their parents using the money to improve their nutrition, regularly bringing their children to physical examinations, and having a high attendance rate in school. By offering financial support under those conditions, the absence rate of schools would be lower, and the educational effectiveness would be better. 

  

Monitoring and Evaluation

The monitoring and evaluation of this project include Randomized Controlled Trials (RCTs) in the pilot phase, automatic learning effectiveness analysis, interviews and surveys, and long-term economic indexes. Those measurements ensure that this project could be executed stably, effectively, and sustainably.

Firstly, during the pilot phase, RCTs would be used to evaluate the effectiveness of the new project by comparing two similar regions. One of them introduces the project. At the same time, the other one continues the traditional teaching method. Three years later, the standardized test scores, attendance rates, and other key data between the two groups were compared to examine the efficiency of this project. This project could expand to other areas better by amplifying its advantages and alleviating its disadvantages.

Second, students' laptops could automatically record the learning duration, performance, and other data. Based on those data, the system will automatically analyze them and generate reports about students' use of the learning platform, which will be uploaded to their teachers regularly. In this way, teachers could monitor the performance and progression of students quickly, and give some students in need special tutoring, and make sure that no one student is left behind.

Third, conducting interviews and surveys with teachers, students, and parents involved in the project is indispensable. The purpose is to understand the concerns and hidden issues in diverse local contexts and address them periodically. From the teachers' perspective, we will know the real challenges of teaching in this project. From the parents' perspective, we could get an insightful view of the change in students in their families, which could not be observed in the class. From the students’ perspective, their needs and feelings could be understood better. Combining those concerts, this project could be more flexible and suitable for each rural area. 

Finally, long-term Indicators, such as enrollment rates, employment rates, and per capita GDP of rural areas, will be the key indexes of the long-term evaluation of this project. Theoretically, higher school attendance rates and better educational quality will contribute to the well-rounded development of students and prepare them for a decent job in the future. Thus, the employment rate would increase, and the local socioeconomic status would be enhanced. Those improvements could be observed when the project is executed for over twenty years through pre-post comparison.

  

Limitations and Challenges

Finally, I acknowledge that there are several remaining limitations and challenges. Particularly cramming education, teacher retention rates, and sustainability. 

Cramming education is a challenging issue in educational support. Notably, in China, education's primary goal has already been improving the grades on standardized tests to enter a better high school or university, regardless of students' mental health and holistic development. Introducing laptops, online education platforms, and AI tools for learning in rural areas may exacerbate the vicious competition of cramming education. Despite the improvement in educational quality, the difficulty of entering a university will not be changed. In this way, students will be forced to focus only on learning for exams solely, even in this project. One possible and ultimate solution is to diversify the way of admission and further develop this project to establish high schools and universities in rural areas.

The low teacher retention rate is another tricky challenge. A previous study shows that 71.56% of rural teachers in China want to turnover from rural schools to urban schools  (Wang and Wu, 2019). According to another research, low salaries, inadequate facilities, lousy working conditions, and insufficient job satisfaction are highly related to the quit of rural teachers. (Cheng, Diao, & Ding, 2023) Another factor is that this project's teachers will suffer from a lot of pressure to manage students and schools. Therefore, improving the teacher retention rate must be taken into consideration. A possible solution might be hiring fewer teachers but offering better working conditions and salaries.

Sustainability is the most tricky problem for this project, including sustainability of policy and finance. On the one hand, based on the bureaucratic system of China, the goal of every official, including the leader of the educational department, is to complete the KPI to be promoted to a higher position. Thus, those bureaucracies in government who may be in charge of this project do not really care about the educational support project, and their policy on supporting the project will cease when they are promoted. (Mei & Zhai, 2018)  On the other hand, financial sustainability must also be considered carefully. This project is long-term, so it requires long-term stable investment in rural areas to cover the costs of facilities, salaries, financial aid, etc. Besides the instability of the bureaucratic system, corruption, improper distribution of finance, and insufficient investment are possible reasons for the economic challenge. A potential solution could be establishing a transparent funding system trusted by the public to ensure the proper use of money in this project and build up reciprocal relationships with social organizations and enterprises.

  

Conclusion

In conclusion, the rural educational support in modern China project is a feasible idea to break the cycle of poverty from the point of education by introducing modern technology, hiring a few high-quality teachers, and offering financial support based on collaboration with government, NGOs, and enterprises. The key difference between traditional rural educational support and this project is that this project embraces modern technology, including artificial intelligence, online education software, and platforms, to address the issue of shortage of teachers and the disparity of educational resources between rural and urban areas. Thus, teachers’ role in this project shifts from an imparter of knowledge to an encourager and guide for students’ holistic development.

Regarding the measurements of monitoring and evaluation, the basic idea is to combine short-term data and long-term indexes to evaluate the efficiency of this project, covering educational and economic indicators. Even though there are a few challenges, including cramming education, teacher retention rate, and the sustainability of policy and finance, some possible solutions exist to alleviate the competition, improve the teacher's job sufficiency, and ensure financial transparency.
