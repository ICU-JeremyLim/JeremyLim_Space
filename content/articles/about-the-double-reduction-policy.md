Introduction: The background of the Double Reduction policy

Even though the Double Reduction Policy is not a literal educational project or program, it is one of the most impactful education policies on compulsory education in mainland China in the 21st century, and it includes many project-like measurements and details for its implementation. This article will introduce the background of this policy and briefly describe its implementation, as well as the evaluation and different opinions on it. Finally, I will conclude with my recommendation. 

Similar to Korea, Japan, and other East Asian countries, one of the most serious issues of education in mainland China is the over-competition in cramming the educational system, which led to widespread reliance on private tutoring institutions, such as New Oriental and Histudy, resulting in academic pressure on students and financial stress on families. This issue also exacerbated the inequality between high-income and low-income families and the urban-rural disparity, leading to class solidification because wealthy families could offer better educational resources for their children. Another derived issue is the high financial burden of education on each family, which contributed to the decline in the total fertility rate of mainland China from 2.6% in 1982 to 1.6% in 2015 because it is evident that the decrease in fertility rate corresponding to the increase in educational competition and the booming of private tutoring company, as well as the widespread of cramming education culture in recent forty years.

According to the official document YiJian 2021 (General Office of the CPC Central Committee & General Office of the State Council, 2021), based on the comprehensive considerations of the Chinese government, the Double Reduction Policy (双减政策), issued by the General Office of the Central Committee of the Communist Party of China and the State Council, implemented in July 2021 by the Chinese government, aimed at reducing two burdens in primary school and secondary school (compulsory education): reducing after-school private tutoring and reducing excessive homework. This policy aims to enhance the primary role of schools in education, deepen the governance of off-campus training institutions, and promote the holistic development and healthy growth of students. (YiJian, 2021)

Description: The implementation of the Double Reduction Policy

According to YiJian 2021, the implementation of the double reduction policy involved multi-layer strategies. In this essay, I will divide them into two levels: off-school and in-school.

As for the implementation of the off-school level, several rigid rules restrict the off-school tutoring institutions. First, no new academic subject-based off-campus training institutions targeting students in compulsory education will be approved in any region. Second,  existing academic training institutions must be uniformly registered as non-profit organizations, and such institutions are prohibited from publicly engaging in capitalized operations. These two regulations demonstrate that by running the private tutoring business, there are no longer any attainable profits, and those institutions have to close or transform to another industry. Third, the limitations of private tutoring, whether private tutoring institutions or private teachers, are highly restrictive. For example, the new rules require all private tutoring teachers must have teacher certification. However, the fact is that teachers with certification could work in public schools and get almost double the salary of private tutoring institutions, so the majority of teachers in private institutions are without teacher certification. Moreover, the primary purpose of students attending after-school classes at private tutoring institutions is to utilize their weekend and summer break to learn in advance, so they can have more time to review the knowledge and achieve higher exam scores. Nevertheless, another restriction is regulating teaching content and teaching time in those private institutions, such as prohibiting learning advanced content and prohibiting institutions from teaching during weekends and holidays, which means that private tutoring institutions can no longer fulfill the needs of students. Consequently, with few exceptions, most tutoring centers were prohibited from making a profit and went bankrupt.

In order to make sure that all of the measures mentioned above are implemented correctly, YiJian 2021 clarifies the responsibilities and obligations of each related department. First, the Public Security Department should eliminate illegal private tutoring institutions. Second, the Market Supervision Department should oversee the registration of non-academic training institutions and regulate off-campus training institutions in areas such as fees and advertising. Third, the education department should establish standards for school after-school service charges and materials.

On the other hand, the in-school side should reduce the pressure on students and bridge the gap of private tutoring institutions at the same time. First, reducing the amount of homework. Schools must ensure that no written homework is assigned to first and second-grade students in primary school. For students in grades three to six and junior high school, written homework should primarily be completed during school hours. Second, the quality of after-school services should be enhanced to meet students’ diverse needs. Free after-school tutoring should be provided by the school’s own teachers, retired teachers, and qualified volunteers. In addition, offering free and high-quality educational resources to all students through online websites is also started.

Instead of direct supervision, the way to make sure those new rules would be implemented on the in-school side is to incorporate the effectiveness of the Double Reduction initiative into the evaluation of education quality at the county and school levels and let the Chinese Communist Party (CCP) organizations in each school carry out ideological work among teachers to motivate and inspire their enthusiasm and creativity fully. It is common sense in China that the indicators of evaluation of education quality impact the promotion prospects of officials in relevant departments and the CCP organization in each school is the most powerful department, which can decide the personnel appointments and financial expenditures.

Evaluation: Different Opinions on the Double Reduction Policy

As for the evaluation part, I collected different opinions from official media, scholars, and commentators, as well as some regional surveys to reflect the effectiveness of the Double Reduction policy.

The People's Daily, the official newspaper of the [Central Committee of the Chinese Communist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Committee_of_the_Chinese_Communist_Party) (CCP), highly appreciated this policy, stating that “Since the implementation of the Double Reduction policy, the burden of off-campus tutoring has decreased, repetitive homework assigned by schools has been reduced, children are getting more sleep” in 2022 and “The implementation of the Double Reduction policy has significantly advanced the process of education modernization in multiple aspects, laying a solid foundation for cultivating well-rounded socialist builders and successors who excel morally, intellectually, physically, aesthetically, and in work skills.”  in 2024. However, official departments did not offer any national data to evaluate the effectiveness of this policy.

Although there is no national data for the results of the implementation of this policy, the Beijing city government reported that in Beijing, 98% of unlicensed private tutoring institutions and 60% of legal private tutoring institutions closed in 2021, and the Chengdu city government stated that the after-school service coverage rate for compulsory education schools in the city has reached 100%. Moreover, psychological research shows that the prevalence of depression (9.9% vs. 9.4%) and anxiety (7.4% vs. 7.1%) slightly decreased after the policy was implemented. The Double Reduction Policy seems successful in meeting its goal, at least in some areas.

Nevertheless, there are some criticisms of this policy. Li, a commentator in mainland China, pointed out that from the perspective of reducing students' academic burden, the policy is undoubtedly a failure: first, qualified private tutoring institutions have decreased, but underground one-on-one tutoring has surged, increasing fees and parents' financial burdens; second, local education departments prioritize political achievements, such as enrollment rate for top universities, ignoring schools that fail to implement the policy. For example, many schools, to maintain their average grades, still require students to arrive as early as 7 a.m., and some schools even use dual timetables—one for inspections from higher authorities and another for their actual daily teaching.

Furthermore, this policy derived some issues in other aspects as well. First, the burdens of teachers on teaching were increased. For instance, according to a survey ([Li et al., 2023](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/20965311241265123?icid=int.sj-full-text.similar-articles.1#bibr7-20965311241265123)) of over 9,000 teachers who are involved in after-school service in public schools, 91.3% reported prolonged working time and increased workload, and 51% of teachers felt overstressed. Second, a large amount of staff became unemployed overnight due to the Double Reduction policy, and relevant departments have yet to coordinate efforts to absorb the unemployment caused by the withdrawal of capital from the education and training sector.

Therefore, although there are several achievements of the Double Reduction Policy, several issues need to be solved to ensure that this policy is sustainable and could benefit students and teachers really.

  
  

Recommendations and Conclusion

I have several recommendations that might be helpful in solving the remaining issues of the Double Reduction Policy, involving the root of cramming education and the optimization of educational resources.

Firstly, the foundational problem is the cramming education culture derived from the current educational system. The cramming education culture has been rooted in people’s hearts since the Tang Dynasty because the national exam is the only way to enter top universities and attain a decent job, thus overcoming the disparity between social classes. To solve those issues, such as students' mental health and illegal ont-to-one higher price private tutoring mentioned above, it is necessary to cut down the root of the cramming education culture. On the one hand, the government needs to diversify the approaches to entering universities instead of using the national admission exam as the only way. For example, apart from the general entrance examination, some universities in Japan are executing the admissions office entrance examination (AO) to enroll more diverse students who may not be good at taking exams but excel at critical thinking. Moreover, it is also helpful to consider other aspects of performance in the admissions process, such as personal skills and leadership, rather than only focus on the grades. On the other hand, reconstructing the vocational educational system in mainland China is an urgent affair. The current situation is that almost all students think that the only way to get success is to attend a top university. In this way, many vocational colleges cannot hire enough students, thus facing crises, such as financial issues. However, instead of many intelligent leaders, lawyers, or teachers, Chinese society needs more skillful laborers to support the manufacturing industry. Influenced by the cramming education culture, too many university students who graduated from universities could not find a decent job because the market for high-paying jobs has already saturated, but they are unwilling to be average workers or laborers. Therefore,  reconstructing the vocational educational system like that of Germany is significant in both alleviating the competition for university admission and in balancing the proportion of academic students and vocational students to fit the social situation.

Secondly, the disparity between different areas could be solved by optimizing educational resources. On the one hand, many NGOs for teaching in underdeveloped areas in China, such as Teaching For China (TFC), could consider hiring a great amounts of layoff teachers from previous private tutoring institutions to support teaching in underdeveloped areas, such as Xizang Province. In this way, the unemployment issue and the disparity issue could be alleviated simultaneously. Because those teachers from private tutoring institutions have already been equipped with excellent teaching skills to fulfill the diverse needs of students previously, through short-term training of NGOs or NPOs, they could definitely be conducive to teaching children in remote areas, and they could get decent jobs. On the other hand, incorporating Artificial intelligence into national online educational resources is an effective way to increase the equality of education since many students in rural areas can use smartphones to learn knowledge, which could bridge the gap of disparity of educational resources.

In conclusion, the original intention of the Double Reduction Policy is conducive to reducing the pressure on students from both in-school and off-school sides, and it is still impactful, at least in some areas of mainland China. However, the government tends to appreciate the policy made by itself but does not research or publicize the research of the effectiveness of the policy in the national range. Through some surveys and comments from scholars, it is evident that the implementation processes are insufficient and imperfect, which also causes several derivational issues. The key to solving the problem of the educational field of China is to cut off the roots of the cramming education culture, as I mentioned above. I believe that by optimizing the educational resources and diversifying the approaches of university admission, students in primary and secondary school could enjoy a less stressful life and compulsory education in mainland China would be better.
